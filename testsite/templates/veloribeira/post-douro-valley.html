Riding the Douro Valley: a 3-day itinerary

The Douro Valley is two hours from Porto by train, and it feels like another country: terraced vineyards climbing straight out of the river, quiet roads and a lunch stop at every bend. This is the three-day loop we recommend to customers who want to ride it on their own. It's about 85 km in total, with one hard day in the middle.

Before you go

You'll want low gears and a rack. You can rent one of our touring bikes for €25 a day with panniers included, and drop it at Régua station at the end if you'd rather not bring it back on the train. Pack light: read our guide to packing panniers before you leave, and take a spare tube whatever bike you ride.

Trains from São Bento to Peso da Régua leave roughly every two hours. Bikes travel free outside rush hour, but there are only a few spaces on each train, so get there early.

Day 1: Peso da Régua to Pinhão (25 km)

An easy first day on the N222, which follows the south bank of the river and is often called one of the best driving roads in the world. On a bike you'll have it mostly to yourself before 10:00. Stop at the viewpoint above Folgosa, and again in Folgosa itself for lunch. We've posted the route on PedalMap: Day 1 route on PedalMap.

Spend the night in Pinhão. It's small, so book ahead; we've listed our favourite guesthouses in where to stay in Pinhão.

Day 2: Pinhão to São João da Pesqueira and back (32 km)

The hard day: around 900 metres of climbing to the São Salvador do Mundo viewpoint and the town of São João da Pesqueira, then a long, fast descent back to the river. Leave early to avoid the afternoon heat, and carry more water than you think you need. Day 2 route on PedalMap.

Day 3: wine, then the train home

Ride back towards Régua at your own pace and stop at a quinta on the way. If you'd like someone else to organise the tasting, Douro Ride's bike and wine tour visits two quintas and includes lunch (their site is in Portuguese, but they speak English). The afternoon train gets you back to São Bento in time for dinner.

Rather not plan it yourself?

Our guided tours take care of the route, the bags and the lunch stops. The closest in spirit is the Gerês national park weekend: two days of quiet mountain roads north-east of Braga.
