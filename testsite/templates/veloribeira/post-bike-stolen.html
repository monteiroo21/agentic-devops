Bike stolen in Porto? What to do next

It happens to about a dozen of our customers every year, usually outside a café or a metro station. If you come back and your bike is gone, here's what to do, in this order.

1. Check it wasn't moved

Bikes locked to railings near building works or market stalls are sometimes moved by the council. Ask in the shops nearby before you assume the worst.

2. Report it to the police

Report the theft at the nearest PSP station or online through the Queixa Eletrónica service. You'll need the frame number, so take a photo of it today, before you need it. Renting from us? Call us first: we'll give you the frame number and help with the report.

3. Search the second-hand listings

Stolen bikes often reappear on online marketplaces within a week. Search for your model every day and set up alerts. If your bike is not found in the first two weeks, it has usually left the city.

4. Tell your insurer

Most home insurance covers bikes kept at home but not bikes stolen in the street, so check your policy. If the bike can't be found, you'll need the police report number to make a claim.

How to make it less likely

  • Use a D-lock through the frame and back wheel, plus a cable for the front wheel.
  • Lock it to something that can't be lifted or cut, in a busy, well-lit place.
  • Write down the frame number and keep the receipt.

Every rental from us comes with a D-lock, and we're happy to show you how to use it.
